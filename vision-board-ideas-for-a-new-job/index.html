Vision board ideas for a new job

A job search is mostly waiting, and a vision board is a good way to keep the waiting pointed somewhere. A new job is different again: the first months are when you decide what kind of colleague you are going to be.

Here are ideas for both: the board you make while you are looking, and the one you make once you have started.

Written by the person who makes Mira · Updated October 2026

While you are looking

Picture the job in enough detail that you would recognise it when it comes up.

  • The kind of work you want to spend your days on
  • The team or company culture you are after, from people who work there
  • The commute, or the home office, you would like
  • A salary figure you have researched
  • A small weekly target: applications sent, people contacted

If you are changing careers

A career change needs a bridge, and the board should show it: the course, the side project or the first small paid job in the new field.

  • The qualification or portfolio piece you need
  • Someone who made the same switch
  • Your first project in the new field, however small

Once you have started

  • The first thing you want to deliver in your first ninety days
  • The people you want to get to know
  • A skill the role will push you to learn
  • How you want to feel on a Friday evening

Lines to pair with it

Keep the words short and in the present tense, so they read as fact rather than wish. A few examples from Mira’s career phrases:

  • Being new is not being less
  • I ask the question I think is dumb
  • I belong in the room
