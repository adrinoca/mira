Vision board ideas for 2027

Most vision boards go quiet for a boring reason: they are a pile of pictures with no structure, so nothing on them asks anything of you. The fix is to sort the board by area of life, give each area a few pictures, and know what each one is for.

Below are eight areas, with what to look for in each. You don’t need all of them: three or four that matter to you in 2027 make a better board than eight that don’t.

Written by the person who makes Mira · Updated September 2026

Choose your areas first

Look back over the last few weeks and ask where your attention actually went: work, a person, a trip, money, sleep. Those are the areas to give room to. An area you have to invent a reason for will look fine on the board and mean nothing by March.

For each area you keep, choose two or three pictures and one short line. A photo on its own stops meaning much within a few weeks; the line is what reminds you why you picked it.

Love and relationships

Boards go wrong here when they are about a person you haven’t met. Pictures of the feeling you want last longer than pictures of a wedding.

  • A table laid for two, or a long dinner with friends
  • Hands: around a mug, mid-conversation, on a shoulder
  • A photo you already have of someone laughing
  • A slow Sunday, the way you would like more of them to go
  • A place you could walk to with someone, or on your own

Money

The pictures that work here are of what the money is for and of the habit that gets you there, not of piles of cash.

  • The thing you are saving for: a deposit, a course, a trip, a workshop
  • A number, written on a card, that you can check against
  • A jar, a notebook or a spreadsheet: the tool you will actually use
  • Your own work at its best: the desk, the bench, the finished project
  • A photo of somewhere that feels like enough

Health

Skip the before-and-after photos. What you are looking for is the everyday version: what a good week of looking after yourself looks like.

  • A walking route you like, in the light you like it in
  • A kitchen table with real food on it
  • A bed made properly, and a lamp switched off on time
  • Running shoes by the door, or a class you want to try
  • A photo of you outside on a day you felt well

Career and work

A career board is easier to write about the next step than about the top of the ladder. What does a good ordinary day of work look like next year?

  • Your desk, or the room you would like to work in
  • The thing you want to finish or launch, drawn or named
  • Someone whose work you admire, and a piece of it
  • The city, company or course, if this is about a move
  • A photo of a moment you were proud of at work

Travel

A destination is a start, but the pictures that pull hardest are of what you would be doing there: the small, specific thing.

  • The place itself, in the season you would go
  • A market, a swim, a train window: the thing you want to do there
  • A map with the route marked, or a booking you have half made
  • A photo from an old trip you want to get back to
  • A packed bag by a door

Home

Choose one room rather than the whole house. A board of a single room’s light, colours and objects is easier to act on than a mood board of a mansion.

  • The room you would change first, as you would like it
  • Light coming through a window at the time of day you are home
  • A colour, a material or a piece of furniture you want
  • A photo of the street or the view you want to live with
  • One object you could buy this month

Calm

This is the area people leave off, and the one whose absence shows first. It is also the easiest to picture, because it is mostly empty space.

  • An empty bench, a quiet street, a still lake
  • A closed laptop on a tidy desk
  • A slow morning: a window, a cup, no phone in the frame
  • A path through trees
  • Somewhere you have felt unhurried, from your own photos

Personal growth

Growth boards work best when they show the practice and not the result: the instrument, the book, the trail, not the applause.

  • A book open on a table, or a stack you plan to read
  • The tool of the skill you are learning: a guitar, a sketchbook, a pair of running shoes
  • A calendar page with a streak on it
  • A photo of you a year ago, to look back at later
  • A trail or a staircase going up

Then put it where you will see it

A board on a wall is seen a few times a day at first, and after a month hardly at all. A phone is different: you look at it dozens of times a day without deciding to. Mira, the app this site belongs to, turns a board into a widget: each photo comes with a phrase, and they take turns on your Home Screen and Lock Screen.

How to make a vision board on your iPhone →

Start with three pictures

You don’t need a finished board to begin. Choose an area, add two pictures and a line, and see whether it still means something in a week. Add the next area when it does.
