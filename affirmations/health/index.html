Health affirmations

Health affirmations tend to go wrong in one of two ways: they promise a body you don’t have yet, or they sound like a lecture. Both are easy to tune out. “I am perfectly healthy” is a hard thing to say to yourself in the week you aren’t.

The 25 health phrases in Mira aim lower and closer. Many name a single small act, like standing up, drinking water or taking the stairs, and the rest are about how you treat the body you have. They are grouped into four: the body you have, moving a little, eating and drinking, and rest.

The body you have

The framing group, and the place to start if you are tired, unwell or fed up with your body. The wording is deliberately gentle: “kind”, “gently”, “at my own speed”. “My health is not a punishment” is there for anyone who has turned looking after themselves into a chore, and “I book the appointment I’ve avoided” is the one line here that asks for something today.

Pairs with quiet, unposed pictures: a robe on a hook, light across a bed, a hand around a warm mug.

Moving a little

Deliberately small. “Stand up and stretch right now” is a thirty-second job, taking the stairs is a choice you make once, and “I show up, not perfectly” gives you permission to do half of what you planned. “Strength is built slowly” is for the weeks when nothing seems to show.

Pairs with ordinary movement rather than gym photos: a walking path, running shoes by a door, a yoga mat rolled out.

Eating and drinking

None of these mention weight, calories or willpower. They are about small, kind choices: something green on the plate, a glass of water, a proper lunch break. “I eat like someone I love” is the one to read at the moment you are about to skip a meal.

Pairs with a table set for lunch, a bowl of something fresh, a glass of water in daylight.

Rest and sleep

Rest is where a lot of people give up the most, so this is the biggest group after the first. Several are decisions with a time on them, “before I am wrecked”, “before bed”, and “breathe out longer” and “unclench my jaw” are physical cues you can follow where you stand. “I put the phone down before bed” is best read earlier in the evening.

Pairs with low light and soft edges: a lamp on at dusk, a made bed, a folded blanket.

Pick the smallest one

Choose the line you could act on in the next hour, not the most ambitious one. A phrase that says stand up and stretch does its job the moment you read it. Mira keeps it on your Home Screen with any photo you like.
