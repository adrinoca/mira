Vision board ideas for teens

At fifteen or seventeen, a lot of what is on your mind is decided by other people: timetables, exams, what everyone expects you to do next. A vision board is one of the few places where the plan is entirely yours.

Keep it honest. If you would rather learn to skate than get top marks in chemistry this year, the board should say so.

Written by the person who makes Mira · Updated October 2026

School, your way

  • The subject you actually enjoy, and what you want to do with it
  • A study spot that is just yours
  • The exam or project you most want to feel good about

Friends and the people around you

  • A photo with the friends who make you feel like yourself
  • Something you want to do together this year: a gig, a trip, a club
  • A person you would like to get to know better

Things you want to get good at

This is often the best part of a teen board, because progress is fast and easy to see.

  • An instrument, a sport, a game, drawing, coding, cooking
  • Someone who does it brilliantly
  • A goal you can tick off: a grade, a match, a finished piece

Feeling good in yourself

Pick pictures of how you want to feel, not how you want to look.

  • A place where you feel calm
  • Sleep, water, being outside: the boring things that make every day better
  • A short line you would like to believe about yourself

What comes next

One or two pictures of the future are enough: a course, a city, a job you are curious about. They will change, and that is fine.

Make it and keep it

Print a template and glue in photos, or keep the board on your phone. Mira puts your photos and words on your Home Screen and Lock Screen, one at a time.

Free printable 2027 templates →

Change it whenever you like

A teen board should change as often as you do. Swap pictures out every few months and keep the old ones; they are fun to look back on.
